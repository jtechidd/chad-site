Chinese is my third language (the first two languages are Thai and English), and I think these are the only languages I could learn in my life. I used to study Simplified Chinese at a Chinese school since I was a child. Because I studied at a Chinese school and Chinese classes took up a major part of my schedule, I think I was more fluent in Chinese than English at the time. Even to these days, I still remember some words and how to speak. I studied at a Chinese school until grade 9, then moved to another upper secondary school, a public school that focuses more on scientific knowledge.

Now, I am 26, and I have a deep intuition that I am going to use this language somehow, so I started learning it again, just started in June this year. This time, I will focus only on Traditional Chinese (Taiwan Huayu) characters only, so that I will not hesitate about whether to remember the simplified one or traditional one. I have no trouble learning it because I am familiar with the Chinese language system itself. These are my thoughts on Traditional Chinese:

- Traditional characters contain a lot of strokes, some of the characters are completely different from the simplified characters, and are diabolically challenging to remember, such as 聽(听), 舊(旧), 龍(龙), 頭(头), 憂鬱(忧郁, I am actually depressed learning this).
- Writing is difficult because some characters squeeze vertically, making some characters a little bit higher than others, such as 幫(帮), 邊(边), 穩(稳).
- I thought Japanese Kanji is completely 100% Traditional Chinese characters, but it turns out some characters are actually simplified, such as the word *Learn*, in Japanese it is 学ぶ (Manabu), meanwhile in Traditional Chinese it has some fancy cap like this: 學.
- Because Chinese characters are [logogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logogram) (characters are drawing shape from real world object), I feel that Traditional characters are more consistent, and I think the shape or radical has clearer meaning than the simplified (sometimes).
- Some Taiwan Huayu words are actually different from Mainland China, such as *Traffic jam*, they use 塞車 instead of 堵車, or *Hotel*, they use 旅館 instead of 酒店. Some words have the same writing but are pronounced in a difference way, such as 垃圾 (rubbish), pronounced as lèsè instead of lājī.
- I feel calligraphy in Traditional Chinese characters is more magnificent, more like a piece of art.

Unfortunately, there are a few books that write about Traditional Chinese. Most of the Chinese books in the bookstore are written for Simplified Chinese learners. So I learn Traditonal Chinese mainly from the internet, and it is essential to read from Taiwanese websites directly. Though I can use a Chrome extension to substitute simplified characters with Traditional characters, there are still nuances between these two similar languages.

Here are some sources I used to study Traditional Chinese:

- [Taiwan Center for Mandarin Learning](https://taiwancenter.taiwan-world.net/material) This is the main source for my learning, there are several books for different levels, and those books are very high quality (with very high file size).
- [Taiwanese Mandarin With Miss Lin](https://www.taiwanesemandarinwithmisslin.com/) Her blogs are good to read and easy to follow. You can learn about Taiwanese culture from her blogs, and you can even checkout her courses teaching Taiwanese Mandarin.
- [方格及 vocus](https://vocus.cc) I sometimes read posts and articles from several Taiwanese people there.

Since Chinese is a language that has a large number of characters, I am also using a tool like [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) to build flashcard decks to help me remember a lot of words, phrases, and sentences (I will cover this later). Like learning other languages in general, I don't focus much on the grammar, but just train my brain to be able to speak or write instantly.

Despite its difficulty, I still benefit from learning Traditional Chinese in some ways, such as I can recognize some words that appear in some sacred/traditional areas, like temples or shrines, or I can recognize a lot of Kanji characters now (though I still pronounce them in Chinese, what a shame).

That's it for now. I will keep grinding until I have enough confidence in this language again. After that, I will find an opportunity to take the TOCFL test. Wish me luck.
